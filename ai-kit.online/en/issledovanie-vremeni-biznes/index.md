## Where the time comes from

Someone who works for themselves does two jobs. The first is the one they get paid for: a consultation, a deal, a lesson, a product. The second is everything around it: replying in three messengers, entering a request, issuing an invoice, compiling a report, checking competitors. Surveys of entrepreneurs agree: this second job takes from 8 to 20-plus hours a week.

We count only the digital part: what is done in email, messengers, spreadsheets, accounting software and on websites. Calls, in-person meetings and the work with the client itself are not counted. A working year is 247 working days and 48 working weeks.

We took three scenarios. An expert with an audience — an online school, a blog, consultations; works alone or with one or two people. A small business owner — up to 15 people, handles sales and administration personally. A private practitioner — a realtor, lawyer, consultant, works alone. ## How we calculated

The method in six steps
- We took surveys of entrepreneurs on how much time goes into administrative work and separated out its digital part.
- For each of the 17 activities we estimated minutes per occurrence and frequency for three scenarios. A working year is 247 working days and 48 weeks. Rare tasks, such as hiring, are counted in hours per year.
- For each option we estimated what share of the time it removes, and multiplied it by the share of cases where it is actually used. A chatbot and a paid assistant work only on request: they are used in 60–85% of daily cases. Single-purpose services and the [System](https://ai-kit.online/)'s schedule run on their own — 80–100% and 85–100%.
- We subtracted the time spent on the tool itself: learning it, checking answers, confirmations, moving data between services. For the System in the first year this is 25–55 hours depending on the scenario, afterwards 15–40.
- The range is the spread from a cautious to a bold estimate. We take its midpoint as the expected value.
- Work that one person could not have done alone is counted separately, as the time the same coverage would take by hand. It is not included in the savings.

Cross-check with surveys: our model against the sources

The surveys count all administrative work, including calls and paperwork; we count only the digital part. The model is at the upper edge of the surveys: 15–18 hours a week against 8–16 in foreign surveys — closer to the Russian one, where 43% of owners spend more than 20 hours on such tasks.
